## 1. Executive summary

Since the July audit, two of its 17 documented stub modules — `BountySystem` and `RivalrySystem` —
went from `function X.Init(): () end` to real ~200-line implementations, alongside a new client
`BountyMenu` scaffold and a server→client kill feed. All eight of July's confirmed combat defects
(D1–D8) remain correctly fixed with **no regressions** — verified fresh against current source, not
assumed from commit messages.

The new work is a mixed picture. `RivalrySystem` correctly adopted the `GameplayEvents`
inverted-dependency pattern July's audit prescribed (3.1) — but in doing so **reintroduced the exact
unbounded player-reference leak class that audit's own D8 fix had just eliminated elsewhere**, this
time with no bound and no `PlayerRemoving` cleanup at all. `BountySystem` has a more serious integrity
gap: its `ClaimBounty` function accepts a claimer identity as a bare argument with **no verification
that the claimer actually killed the target** — currently harmless only because no RemoteEvent exists
for it yet, but it is the entire trust boundary the moment one does, and it directly contradicts this
game's stated "power is earned in combat, not claimed" design pillar.

A fresh combat pass surfaced one balance-relevant defect the July audit's fixes exposed but didn't
create: **Primary's two-stage Heavy combo is now mathematically reachable (D4 fixed that) but
practically unlandable against any attentive opponent** — its 3.00s cooldown leaves the target over
two full seconds of free action before the attacker is even permitted to throw the second hit, unlike
Secondary's already-correct Heavy string. A security sweep of every non-combat system the July audit
never examined (Moderation, ServerHop, BugReport, CharacterCreation, AdminAction, PlayerData, and a
sample of DevMenuSystem's 37 RemoteFunctions) found **no privilege-escalation paths** — every admin
action re-checks its whitelist per call — but did find a real double-finalize race in character
creation.

**The single most consequential finding for planning:** Bounty and Rivalry could ship ahead of the
documented Tier 4 order only because they needed nothing but `GameplayEvents` (3.1). That head start
is also their ceiling — every payout destination they compute a reward for (`RewardSystem`,
`MeridianSystem`, `TierSystem`) is still a stub. They currently produce correctly-shaped reward
manifests with nowhere to deposit them. This raises the priority of the Meridian/Tier spine; it does
not reduce it.

## 3. New defects — Tier 1 candidates

Ordered by severity. All independently shippable; none require Tier 3 structural work first.

### 3.1 `BountySystem.ClaimBounty` has no kill-verification — Critical

`BountySystem.lua:183-202`. `ClaimBounty(bountyId, claimer)` accepts `claimer: Player` as a bare
argument and simply records it — nothing checks the claimer defeated the target, or that the target
died at all. This is currently unreachable from any client (no RemoteEvent exists — confirmed, see
§7.1), so it is not live-exploitable today, but it is the entire trust boundary the instant one does,
and the game's own design pillar ("progression that can be satisfied without fighting another player
is a bug, not a feature") makes this a correctness issue, not just a hardening one.

**Recommendation:** don't make `ClaimBounty` directly callable at all. Mirror `RivalrySystem`'s
already-correct pattern exactly — `BountySystem.Init()` subscribes to `GameplayEvents.OnPlayerKilled`
(`RivalrySystem.lua:114-118` is the template), and when a victim matches an active bounty's
`targetName`, the confirmed killer automatically becomes `claimedBy`. No manual claim path, no
identity to spoof.

### 3.2 `RivalrySystem` — unbounded `Player`-reference leak, live-wired to every PvP kill — High

`RivalrySystem.lua:24-30, 38`. `rivalryStandings`/`playerScores`/`playerEntries` are keyed by
`tostring(player)` and never trimmed — confirmed via grep, **zero** `Players.PlayerRemoving`
references in the file. `RegisterPvPKill` is not dormant: `Init()` subscribes to
`GameplayEvents.OnPlayerKilled` (`:114-118`), so this runs on every real PvP death server-wide from
the moment `Main.server.lua` boots. This is structurally the same leak class as July's D8, except D8
was bounded (max 4 stale entries per player, `Constants.Combat.MaxTrackedOpponents`) and this is not.
Estimated impact at realistic churn (200 unique visitors over a 6-hour session) is sub-megabyte —
genuinely small next to the game's other memory costs — but the pattern is wrong and was just copied
once already (§3.5).

**Recommendation:** key all three tables by the `Player` instance or `UserId` directly (not
`tostring(player)`), and add a `Players.PlayerRemoving` hook mirroring
`clearRecentOpponentReferencesTo` (`CombatSystem.lua:806-810`). ~10 lines, same shape as D8's fix.

### 3.3 `CharacterCreationSystem.handleFinalize` — double-finalize race — Medium

`CharacterCreationSystem.lua:343-447`. The "already onboarded" guard reads `profile.raceId` **before**
the yielding `TextService:FilterStringAsync` call (`:378-390`), and `PlayerDataSystem.Transform`
(`:392-396`, called after the yield) applies unconditionally with no re-check. Two concurrent
`CharacterCreation_Finalize` calls from the same client can both pass the guard, both yield, and both
call `Transform` — the second silently overwrites the first's `raceId`/`displayName`/`attributes`
(last-write-wins). Not a privilege-escalation vector (both payloads are independently
budget-validated), but it violates the module's own stated invariant and doubles side effects (two
`PivotTo`s, two unfreeze calls). `BugReportSystem.Submit` already closes the identical race correctly
by setting its cooldown flag **before** its own yielding call (`BugReportSystem.lua:231-232`) — use
that as the fix template.

**Recommendation:** add an eager per-player "finalizing" flag, set before the
`TextService:FilterStringAsync` call, same shape as `ServerHopSystem.pendingByPlayer`.

### 3.4 Primary weapon's Heavy2 is reachable but not landable — High (combat balance)

See §6.1 for full analysis. `Constants.lua`'s Primary `Heavy1.Cooldown = 3.00` is nearly triple its
own swing timeline (1.17s) and almost 2.4s longer than the 0.6s hitstun it inflicts — meaning any
attentive opponent has ~2.4 seconds of free action to guard up before the attacker is even allowed to
throw Heavy2. Secondary's equivalent combo (`DaggerHeavy1.Cooldown = 0.58`, matching its own timeline)
works correctly by contrast. D4's fix made Heavy2 state-machine-reachable; it did not make it
fight-reachable.

**Recommendation:** two legitimate directions — (A) cut Primary Heavy1's cooldown toward its own
timeline (~1.2s) so Heavy2 lands inside the target's hitstun window, restoring the "Cooldown ≤
timeline" invariant the rest of `Weapons` already follows; or (B) if the long cooldown is intentional,
stop presenting Heavy2 as a combo continuation and make it a standalone heavy option instead. Leaving
it as-is is worse than the pre-fix dead data, since it now costs the player a telegraphed commitment
for (almost always) nothing against real opposition.

### 3.5 Air-tech counterplay lost if the juggling attacker disconnects mid-hold — Medium

`AirCombo.lua:496-521`, `CombatSystem.lua:2510-2517, 2939-2960`. `findAirComboAttacker` locates "who
is juggling me" via a reverse scan over `combatStates`. `onPlayerRemoving` clears
`combatStates[player] = nil` unconditionally with no equivalent of `clearSuspendedReferencesTo` for
this *active* (non-suspended) relationship. If the juggling attacker disconnects while the victim is
still physically ragdoll-held (`RagdollController.HoldAloft` has its own independent timer, unaffected
by the attacker leaving), every tech attempt is rejected as `"NotJuggled"` until that hold's hard
timer eventually releases them. This is the one documented case where a player who is still physically
held cannot exercise the game's own stated "air-tech must work while ragdolled" invariant — not
infinite helplessness, but a real, silent loss of a skill-based escape, and it's asymmetric (the
departing player's disconnect costs their victim, not them).

**Recommendation:** add a reverse-scan cleanup in `onPlayerRemoving` (same shape as
`clearSuspendedReferencesTo`) that force-releases the hold or grants a synthetic tech success for any
victim currently held as the departing player's `airComboTarget`.

### 5.1 New structural findings

**`DevMenuSystem.lua` monolith — unchanged, untouched by this diff.** Now measured at 1,346 lines / 37
`RemoteFunction` registrations (July measured 1,340/34 — the drift is residue of already-committed
work, not new). `git diff --stat` confirms this file has zero lines touched in the current uncommitted
diff. July's §5.4 finding stands exactly as documented.

**`GameplayEvents` self-subscription in the same module — low, informational.** `CombatSystem.Init()`
now subscribes to its own published `OnPlayerKilled` event to fire the kill-feed remote
(`CombatSystem.lua:3386-3397`), rather than calling it inline from `confirmDeath` the way the adjacent
feedback payload is. Not wrong, but buys none of the decoupling `GameplayEvents` exists for since
publisher and subscriber are the same module — don't treat this as the template for future
same-module producer/consumer pairs.

**No `GameplayEvents` subscriber registry yet — fine at 2 subscribers, won't be at 7+.** Today exactly
2 non-publisher modules subscribe to `OnPlayerKilled` (`RivalrySystem`, and `CombatSystem`'s own
kill-feed handler). July's Tier 4 plan queues up 5-7 more (`QiSystem`, then the full
`Meridian→Tier→Absorb→Reward→Progression` spine) that will all react to a confirmed kill, each
independently guessing ordering/idempotency semantics. **Recommendation:** before that spine lands, add
a running subscriber inventory to `GameplayEvents.lua`'s header — documentation, not new runtime
machinery — so ordering assumptions live in one place instead of archaeology across N `Init()`
functions.

**The three `Server/Managers/` stubs need different treatment, not one shared precedent.**
`ArtTreeManager`/`BloodlineManager` are read-mostly content registries (what arts/bloodlines exist) —
structurally like `Constants.Combat.Weapons`, not per-player state; Bounty/Rivalry's in-memory pattern
is irrelevant to them either way. `FactionManager` is categorically different: its own header states it
owns **membership and standing** — per-player state that must survive a rejoin. **If it's implemented
following Bounty/Rivalry's in-memory-reset-on-`Init()` shape, faction membership is lost on every
server restart** — a materially worse regression than Rivalry's ephemeral standings, since faction is a
foundational identity choice. When implementation starts, `FactionManager` should read/write through
`PlayerDataSystem`'s existing DataStore-backed profile, not invent parallel in-memory state — the
precedent to follow is `PlayerDataSystem`, not `BountySystem`/`RivalrySystem`.

**`BountyMenu.lua` duplicates `BountySystem`'s reward formula with no shared source of truth.**
`BountyMenu.lua:39-45` hand-copies the same six magic constants (125/45/40/18/12/6) from
`BountySystem.lua:55-60`. The file's header frames this as deliberate temporary scaffolding, but
nothing enforces that — no lint rule, test, or owned TODO — and the widget is already mounted live in
`Menus/init.lua:41`, reachable via the `M` keybind, not sitting inert in a branch. This codebase has
already solved this exact problem once: `Shared/FlightMath.lua` extracts pure, Instance-free math
specifically so client and server share one source of truth and it's TestEZ-testable headlessly. The
bounty formula is an even simpler case (three arithmetic expressions, no state).
**Recommendation:** extract to `ReplicatedStorage/Shared/BountyMath.lua`, mirroring `FlightMath.lua`'s
shape, before the menu is ever wired to real server data — once it pulls live bounties, a formula drift
becomes a player-visible reward mismatch, not just a demo-data quirk.

### 5.2 Tier 4.1 (terrain-aware slide) reassessed — still correct, now higher priority, precedent corrected

July's recommended replacement for the `WalkSpeed`-multiplier movement model — a `LinearVelocity`
constraint engaged only during a traversal state, with pure math extracted to `Shared/SlideMath.lua`
mirroring `Shared/FlightMath.lua` — is confirmed still architecturally correct on a fresh read of both
`FlightPhysics.lua` and `FlightMath.lua`.

Two updates:

- **Priority is higher now, not orthogonal to what's shipped.** Bounty and Rivalry are structurally a
  chase-and-flee loop — a marked target has strong incentive to disengage and run across open, sloped
  world terrain, not a flat arena floor. That's precisely the scenario the current WalkSpeed model is
  blind to by construction (no slope response, no momentum carry). The new PvP-incentive layer
  increases how often "does Slide read the terrain" becomes decision-relevant. Recommend bumping 4.1
  above other Tier 4 items once its Tier 3 gates land.
- **The precedent citation needs correcting.** `FlightPhysics.lua`'s rig is entirely client-side by
  design — its own header states flight needs no server module because it's fully client-trusted, with
  "no adversarial relationship to guard against." A server-authoritative Slide (mandatory, per this
  codebase's combat-authority discipline) can't copy that as-is: a player's `HumanoidRootPart` defaults
  to client network ownership, so a server-created `LinearVelocity` constraint would still simulate
  client-side unless ownership is explicitly forced server-side first — exactly the
  `SetNetworkOwner(nil)`/restore-on-exit pattern `RagdollController.lua:143-163, 592, 630` already
  implements for ragdoll/knockback. **The correct precedent is `FlightMath`/`FlightPhysics` for the
  velocity-drive shape, plus `RagdollController` for the ownership-transfer mechanics** — not
  `FlightPhysics` alone, since it solves a different trust problem.

## 6. Combat balance re-check (new — the July audit never covered balance)

Reasoned from `Constants.lua`'s `Combat.Weapons` table and `CombatSystem.lua`'s readiness logic; no
runtime playtest data available, same constraint July's combat specialist worked under.

### 6.1 Primary's Heavy2 — full analysis

| Sequence | Timeline (Windup+Active+Recovery) | Cooldown gate | Damage / Posture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary Heavy1 | 0.60+0.22+0.35 = 1.17s | 3.00s | 12 / 22 |
| Primary Heavy2 | 0.20+0.24+0.40 = 0.84s | 3.00s | 21 / 25 |
| Universal hitstun on a landed hit | — | 0.6s | — |

Heavy1 lands and applies 0.6s of hitstun. Primary's `heavyAttackReadyAt` doesn't clear until 3.00s
after the throw — roughly **2.4 seconds after the target's stun has already worn off**, during which
the defender has a fully free window to block, parry, or disengage. `HeavyComboResetSeconds = 3.5`
(D4's fix) only gives the attacker a 0.5s edge in which Heavy2 is even attemptable at all. Secondary's
equivalent (`DaggerHeavy1.Cooldown = 0.58`, matching its own timeline) has no such gap — it's a
genuine, working hitstun-locked chain.

The `HitStunDuration = 0.6` rationale already documented in `Constants.lua` explicitly treats the
Heavy1→Heavy2 transition gap as "cleared with margin" — that reasoning is correct for Secondary and was
never reconciled with Primary's deliberately-longer cooldown after D4 patched the reachability bug
underneath it. Landing Primary's two-stage Heavy against a real, attentive opponent (this game's stated
audience) will read as "technically possible, practically a trap" — punishing the player for using
authored, intended content.

**Two legitimate fixes** (pick one deliberately):
- **(A)** Cut Heavy1's cooldown toward its own timeline (~1.2s), making Windup the sole commitment cost
  — restores the "Cooldown ≤ timeline" invariant the rest of `Weapons` already follows, and makes the
  combo land the way Secondary's does. Needs a compensating `Recovery` increase so a whiffed/blocked
  Heavy1 stays risky without the long cooldown doing that job.
- **(B)** If the long cooldown is intentional (Heavy1 as an occasional high-value posture tool, not a
  combo opener), remove the combo-index/wraparound machinery and make Heavy2 a standalone option
  instead of "the second hit of a combo you'll basically never land."

**Adjacent, worth preserving explicitly:** Heavy1 alone already has a coherent niche — 22 posture in
one hit is more than any other single attack, including Basic3 (12) or even the Finisher without its 3
prior hits. Whichever direction 6.1 goes, this niche should be a stated design intent, not an accident
of the numbers.

**Verified sound, no new issue:** D5's Finisher stun fix and D6's Secondary windup fix both hold up on
fresh numeric review — the Normal/Uppercut finisher tradeoff is coherent, and Secondary's
higher-posture-per-second-despite-lower-damage identity checks out.

**Follow-up note, not urgent:** a full Basic→Finisher string (4 hits, ~3.0s, reliably landable) deals
48/67 — meaningfully more than even best-case Heavy1+Heavy2 (33/47, rarely landable per above). Worth
revisiting Heavy's raw numbers once 6.1 is resolved, since fixing landability changes what "balanced
relative to Basic" means.

## 7. Bounty/Rivalry as a design system (new — the July audit never covered game design)

### 7.1 The reward pipeline has no verified-kill spine

Covered in full at §3.1. Restated in design terms: `RivalrySystem` got the fight-to-grow wiring
right — every standing update is driven by a real, server-confirmed `GameplayEvents.OnPlayerKilled`.
`BountySystem` didn't — `ClaimBounty` has no relationship to that event at all. Once a remote exists,
the two obvious failure modes are a claimer collecting on a target who's still alive, or two players
colluding on a free payout. This is the single highest-priority fix in this document.

### 7.2 Reward formula scales on self-reported inputs, not verified target state

`CreateBountyDraft(targetName, difficulty, tier)` (`BountySystem.lua:55-72`) takes `difficulty`/`tier`
as caller-supplied numbers — because there's no `TierSystem`/`MeridianSystem` yet to derive them from.
This cuts two ways: the formula already scales *up* with tier, so low-tier bounty-farming isn't
attractive — but nothing auto-generates a bounty on an actually-dominant player, which is the system's
stated design purpose (a visible counter to a snowballing player, so a server doesn't calcify around
one untouchable name). Player-initiated placement alone will always undersample exactly the players
most in need of a check.

**Recommendation:** once `TierSystem` exists, derive `difficulty`/`tier` from the target's real state
rather than accepting it from the placer. Separately, add a **system-funded "Notoriety Bounty"** class,
auto-triggered off `RivalrySystem.GetTopPlayers()` (already implemented, currently unconsumed by
anything) or a kill-streak threshold — this is the mechanism that actually serves the "counter to
dominance" pillar, and it's buildable the moment `CombatChannel` (Tier 3.2) exists, using data already
being tracked today.

### 7.3 Rivalry standing has no consumer beyond an internal leaderboard

`GetTopPlayers()` (`RivalrySystem.lua:78-107`) is read by nothing outside the module. A number alone is
weak retention design for a heavy-PvP game whose target audience is described as caring about tier
lists — and it's invisible in the moment, undercutting the "legible danger" design language already
used for tier/bloodline/aura elsewhere in the UI.

**Recommendation, cheapest first:**
1. Nameplate/HUD rival marker when standing crosses a threshold — pure UI, no new server system, makes
   the already-shipped system legible today.
2. Feed cross-faction rivalry wins into `FactionManager` standing once it exists (the natural
   integration point).
3. Cosmetic-only title/emblem unlocks at standing thresholds.
4. A "rival nearby" HUD ping on proximity/zone overlap — pulls players back into the core loop without
   literal matchmaking, consistent with this being an open-world PvP game.

### 7.4 Both systems are in-memory only — a narrative cost, not just an architecture one

Neither persists (§5.1 covers this from the architecture angle). The design consequence: a rivalry is
supposed to be a grudge that accumulates across sessions ("I've beaten this person four times"). If it
resets every restart, it can never become that — it stays a stat instead of a story.

### 7.5 Feature-gap prioritization — does Bounty/Rivalry change what's next?

**No — the Meridian/Tier spine still has to come first, and shipping Bounty/Rivalry ahead of it raises
the urgency rather than lowering it.** They could skip ahead of July's documented Tier 4 order
precisely because they only needed `GameplayEvents` (3.1), not the progression spine. But that means
their entire value proposition — paying out currency/XP/Meridian/standing — has nowhere to land: every
payout destination (`RewardSystem`, `MeridianSystem`, `TierSystem`, `FactionManager`) is still a stub.
Right now these two systems compute correctly-shaped reward manifests that get discarded.

**Recommended order:**
1. `CombatChannel` (Tier 3.2) — the remaining structural prerequisite; blocks the Bounty/Rivalry
   remotes recommended above.
2. **`MeridianSystem` MVP, ahead of a full `QiSystem`** — subscribe to `GameplayEvents.OnPlayerKilled`
   (the pattern already proven by `RivalrySystem`), award base Meridian XP per confirmed kill, persist
   through `PlayerDataSystem`, expose a read API for `TierSystem`. Deliberately minimal — no Qi types,
   no Deviation — because those are `QiSystem`'s job and `MeridianSystem` doesn't need them to award XP
   for a kill. This is the smallest piece of the spine that turns Bounty/Rivalry's already-computed
   numbers into something real.
3. `TierSystem`, then `RewardSystem`/`AbsorbSystem` — at which point the §7.2/§7.3 hooks become
   buildable, not just designable.

## 8. Security sweep (new — the July audit never covered non-combat systems)

Applied the same lens July's audit used on combat — "is the caller validated server-side, is there
rate limiting, is client data range/type-checked" — to every RemoteEvent/RemoteFunction handler in
`ModerationSystem`, `ServerHopSystem`, `BugReportSystem`, `CharacterCreationSystem`,
`AdminActionSystem`, `PlayerDataSystem`, a representative sample of `DevMenuSystem`'s 37
RemoteFunctions, and the shared `RateLimiter`/`DataStoreRetry`/`NetworkBridge`/`ChangeNotifier`
modules.

**Headline result: no privilege-escalation paths found.** Every sampled `DevMenuSystem` handler
re-checks `AdminConfig.AuthorizedUserIds` and the rate limiter on every single call, not just at
menu-open time. Target resolution always goes through the server-authoritative `player` argument or a
server-side `Players:GetPlayerByUserId` lookup off a `UserId`, never a client-supplied `Player`
reference. `AdminActionSystem` itself performs no authorization by design (it trusts its only caller),
and that trust is honored everywhere it's called.

Concrete findings are listed in §3.6 (items 3.6.3, 3.6.7–3.6.10 originate from this sweep) and §3.3
(the `CharacterCreationSystem` double-finalize race, the most substantive finding of this pass). Two
additional informational notes, not action items:

- `ModerationSystem.IsBanned` fails **open** on a DataStore error (documented, deliberate — a sustained
  outage silently disables ban enforcement for its duration rather than false-locking legitimate
  players out).
- `DataStoreRetry`/`RateLimiter`/`NetworkBridge` were all read for correctness given more systems now
  depend on them — no bugs found in any of the three. `RateLimiter.Clear` is consistently called on
  `PlayerRemoving` by every consumer checked.
